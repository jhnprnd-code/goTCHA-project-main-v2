goTCHA: Behavioral-focused CAPTCHA for low-vision users

Consent version 2026-10-v5.

WHAT IS THIS ABOUT?

I am building a short game that tells people apart from automated scripts, and checking that it works for people with low vision. To do that we need to see how real people play it.

PROTOTYPE LIMITATION

This is a research pilot, not a working security service. The bundled statistical detector was trained on synthetic data and is disabled; an unvalidated rule-based placeholder handles decisions instead. Do not rely on goTCHA to protect accounts, personal information, or other services.

WHAT WILL I DO?

About 5 minutes: one practice level and three short levels (about 20 seconds each). Press the SPACE bar to jump over red blocks. Please play the way you normally would, using your usual screen setup. You don't need to be perfect.

WHAT IS RECORDED?

  • The times you press and release the SPACE bar, and nothing else you type.
  • Technical details: browser name and version, screen size, zoom level, and how smoothly the page ran.
  • How you would describe your vision, which assistive tools you use, and what kind of keyboard.
  • A random participant code. I do NOT record your name, email, audio, or video. IP addresses are not written to the study data files. If you open this page through a third-party tunnel, its provider may process your IP address and connection metadata under its own privacy policy.

TAKING PART AND WITHDRAWING

Taking part is voluntary and you can stop at any time by closing this page. At the end you will get a participant code. If you give that code to John Paraunda before October 21, 2026, your data will be deleted.

RISKS AND BENEFITS

Risks are minimal: a few minutes of screen time and finger use. If you feel any discomfort, stop. There is no payment involved, and our participation helps test whether a CAPTCHA can work for people with low vision.

HOW WILL THE DATA BE USED?

For a second-year undergraduate course project in the BS Computer Science program at Mapúa Malayan Colleges Mindanao. Results will be reported as group statistics. An anonymous copy of the data may be shared with course instructor Sir Clyde Chester Balaman.
